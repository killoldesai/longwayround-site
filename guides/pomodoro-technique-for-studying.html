The Pomodoro technique for studying

Short focused rounds with real rests are an easy way to start and to keep going. Here is how to use them well.

Where it comes from

Francesco Cirillo developed the Pomodoro technique in the late 1980s while he was a university student, using a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. The idea is simple: work with full attention for one round, rest briefly, and repeat.

A study round, step by step

  1. Pick one thing. A chapter, a set of questions or one topic to recall. A vague task makes a vague round.
  2. Run one round. The classic round is 25 minutes. If that feels long, start with 15.
  3. Rest for real. Stand up, drink some water, look out of a window. Leave your phone alone.
  4. Do a quick check. Say aloud what you remember before you look at your notes. This is more useful than rereading.
  5. Repeat. After about four rounds, take a longer rest.

When your mind wanders

Decide in advance what you will do. Write the distraction down for later, move your phone to another room or take three slow breaths, then carry on. Long Way Round lets you set this as an if-then plan for the Work area.

Choosing a round length

Gentle (15 and 3), Classic (25 and 5) and Deep (50 and 10) all work. Begin with the shortest round you will actually finish, then lengthen it when starting feels easy.

Plan reviews, not only sessions

Learning sticks better when you return to it. In the Study area you say how a session went, and the app suggests a review tomorrow, in 3 days or in a week.

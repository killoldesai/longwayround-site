Camino Portugués: 240 km, one session at a time

A pilgrim path from Porto north to Santiago. In Long Way Round the road takes about 80 hours of focus to walk from Porto to Santiago.

Pro road, planned Portugal to Spain. 10 stops. Distances between stops are approximate and drawn from public sources.

Stops and postcards

Porto 0 km

Across the iron bridge. The old city stacks up from the river in tiles and terracotta. You cross a double-decker iron bridge with the wine cellars below, then turn your back on the sea and point north.

The historic centre of Porto is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Barcelos 54 km

The rooster of Barcelos. Every stall seems to sell a painted rooster. The story goes that one stood up on a judge's table and crowed to save a pilgrim, and the town has never let the tale go.

The Barcelos rooster has become a symbol of Portugal.

Ponte de Lima 87 km

The long stone bridge. A long bridge walks you over a slow river lined with trees. The town is proud of how old it is, and it feels like it is in no hurry.

The bridge at Ponte de Lima has a Roman section and a medieval one.

Valença 121 km

Walls above the river. A star-shaped fortress sits on a hill, and just beyond it, across the river, is another country. You climb through the old gate and look at Spain.

Valença is a fortress town that faced Spain across the Minho river for centuries.

Tui 125 km

Over the border. You cross an iron bridge on foot and change country without changing step. The cathedral on the Spanish side has the thick walls of a fortress.

Tui is the first town in Spain on this route.

Redondela 157 km

Town of viaducts. Two tall railway viaducts stride over the rooftops. You walk beneath them on your way through town.

Redondela is crossed by two tall railway viaducts.

Pontevedra 178 km

The scallop chapel. A small chapel in the shape of a scallop shell waits in the old town. Every pilgrim knows the symbol, and here it is a whole building.

The Pilgrim Chapel in Pontevedra is shaped like a scallop shell.

Caldas de Reis 199 km

Warm springs. Warm water flows from a public spring in the middle of town, and walkers stop to feel it run over their hands. The name of the town comes from the springs.

Caldas de Reis is named for its hot springs.

Padrón 219 km

Peppers and a legend. Small green peppers are fried in oil and salt, and the waiter warns you that a few are hot. Nearby is the place where, in the legend, a boat carrying Saint James arrived.

Padrón peppers are famous for the saying that some are hot and some are not.

Santiago de Compostela 240 km

The great square. The towers appear above the roofs and then the whole square opens out. People lie on the stones looking up, some laughing, some quiet. You made it the long way round.

Walkers who cover at least the last 100 km can collect a certificate at the pilgrim office in Santiago.

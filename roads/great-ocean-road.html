Great Ocean Road: 243 km, one session at a time

Surf towns, rainforest and limestone stacks in the sea. In Long Way Round the road takes about 60 hours of focus to walk from Torquay to Allansford.

Pro road, planned Australia. 12 stops. Distances between stops are approximate and drawn from public sources.

Stops and postcards

Torquay 0 km

Where the road begins. Surfboards lean on every wall, and just down the coast are the waves of Bells Beach. Ahead lies a road that returned soldiers built by hand.

The Great Ocean Road was built by returned soldiers as a memorial to those lost in the First World War.

Anglesea 13 km

Kangaroos on the green. Early in the morning the golf course belongs to the kangaroos. They graze between the holes and hardly look up as you walk by.

Kangaroos often graze on the golf course at Anglesea.

Aireys Inlet 25 km

The white lighthouse. A white lighthouse stands on a headland above the sea. You climb the path through windblown scrub and the whole coast lies below you.

The Split Point Lighthouse near Aireys Inlet appeared in the children's TV show Round the Twist.

Lorne 46 km

Ferns and falls. The road squeezes between forest and sea. A short walk inland leads to a waterfall in a cool fern gully, and you stay longer than you meant to.

Erskine Falls is a waterfall in the hills just inland from Lorne.

Apollo Bay 92 km

Harbour under the ranges. Boats rest in the harbour with green hills piled up behind them. You eat something warm and watch the clouds slide in off the sea.

Apollo Bay sits beside the Great Otway National Park.

Cape Otway 125 km

The oldest light. A lighthouse stands on a cliff at the end of a long forest road. Koalas doze in the gum trees along the way, and the wind pushes at you from the south.

The Cape Otway Lightstation, first lit in 1848, is the oldest surviving lighthouse on the Australian mainland.

Twelve Apostles 173 km

Towers in the sea. Pale limestone stacks rise out of the surf, with the sea slowly working at their feet. You stand on the cliff with everyone else and nobody says very much.

The Twelve Apostles were once known as the Sow and Piglets, and there were never twelve of them.

Loch Ard Gorge 181 km

A wreck and two survivors. Stairs lead down to a small beach tucked between cliffs. A ship was wrecked here long ago, and the gorge still carries its name.

Loch Ard Gorge is named for a ship that was wrecked there in 1878.

Port Campbell 185 km

The small bay. A sheltered bay, a jetty and a handful of streets. After the big cliffs it feels like a small room, and your legs are glad of it.

Port Campbell National Park runs along this stretch of coast.

London Bridge 190 km

The bridge that fell. A rock arch once reached out across the water to the outer rock. You look at the gap where the span used to be and think about how the coast keeps changing.

The span of London Bridge that joined the arch to the mainland collapsed in 1990, and two visitors on the outer rock were rescued by helicopter.

Warrnambool 235 km

Whales in winter. Down on the beach there is a lookout for something very large. In winter, great whales come close to shore to rest and raise their young.

Southern right whales visit the coast near Warrnambool in winter.

Allansford 243 km

The end of the road. The road runs out of coast and turns into an ordinary country town beside a river. You made it the long way round.

The Great Ocean Road ends at Allansford, 243 km from Torquay.

How the walk works
